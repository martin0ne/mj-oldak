A pocket is for carrying things you will not need for the next hour. The phone moved in in 2007. Nobody carries keys there anymore.

What follows is five numbers. Each comes from a different registry, each is about an ordinary day. Not one of them is about technology. All of them are about the same thing: several wars fought at once inside one apartment, none of which has ever been named on the news.

## 1. Four hours, fifty-nine minutes

That is the daily screen time reported by the NASK registry “Nastolatki 3.1” for 2025. It is not a figure pulled from the air — it is the hours in which a person of school age reads, watches, compares himself and is compared. It is the time in which he is everywhere and nowhere at once. In 2022 the same registry reported five hours thirty-six minutes. Three years earlier and thirty-seven minutes more. There is no increase. There is a decrease that most commentary ignored, because commentary needs an increase.

The number is inconvenient because it cancels the simplest story. If screens are eating children's lives, they should be eating more of them every year. The registry says they stopped. The internet war is not escalating. It has settled in.

## 2. Fifteen points

PISA 2022, Volume II, describes the gap in mathematics between students who report being distracted by digital devices and students who do not. The gap is about fifteen points. Fifteen points is not an exam catastrophe; it is one answer on the sheet, maybe two. But a score is not divided into lessons — it is the sum of hundreds of interruptions, each of which looks harmless on its own.

A parent who says “put the phone away and do your homework” is saying something true and useless. The child puts the phone away. The interruption in reading the assignment gets repaired only when someone sits down next to him. No registry contains that, because registries count points, not presence.

## 3. Twenty thousand euros

In 2025 the European Court of Human Rights heard the case of A.W. v. Poland. The case concerned the length of court proceedings — the courts took too long, and by the time they ruled, the relationship between a parent and a child had stopped existing. The Court awarded twenty thousand euros.

It is the only number in this set with a docket number and a date. The Court priced a severed relationship with a child at twenty thousand euros. Not a lifetime, not sentiment — a sum that fits into a budget line. The legal war is the most expensive one, because you pay for it in years, and the ruling arrives when there is nothing left to repair.

## 4. Sixty-one percent

The Reuters Digital News Report 2025 reports that in Bulgaria and Croatia roughly 61 to 63 percent of respondents actively avoid the news. Not merely do not read it — deliberately steer around it. This is not fatigue with one topic; it is resignation from an entire category, the way people stop opening envelopes from the bank.

It has to be said precisely: this is data from two countries, not from Poland. But the mechanism needs no translation. A person who stopped reading the news did not stop being a citizen. He decided that the price of attention was higher than the price of ignorance. There is nothing irrational in that trade.

## 5. Forty-five percent

A European Commission survey published in 2025 under the Consumer Scoreboard found that 45 percent of consumers had encountered fraud in the previous twelve months. Not an attempt — fraud that reached them: a call, an email, a message, a fake shop. Nearly one adult in two.

Fraud stopped being an event. It became a service category, with its own logistics and a price per unit. Once you had to know the victim in order to cheat him; now you send a message to a million people and count the percentage. The commercial war is the only one in which the loss can be booked as a fixed cost.

## What the numbers share

Four of these five are not rising. Screen time fell, the mathematics gap fits inside one exercise, news avoidance has flattened at a level hard to call a catastrophe, and forty-five percent is no longer an exception. The only thing rising is the cost carried by the state, because the budget pays for delay, not the man who caused it.

That is the whole mechanism. A catastrophe does not have to get worse in order to continue. It only has to stay at a level that stops surprising anybody. The war in your pocket has no front, no armistice and no veterans, because nobody comes home from it — it is home.

Five numbers. None of them says what to do. All of them say where we are.
